Bioinformatics and Integrated Omics Systems

Tumours, read across their molecular layers.

A computational biology group at the University of Ulsan College of Medicine. We take genome, transcriptome, proteome, phosphoproteome, single-cell and spatial measurements from the same patients, model them jointly rather than one layer at a time, and then test whether what the model finds survives a cohort it was not trained on.

Research

What we study

The work has two halves that feed each other. Characterisation measures what is present in a tumour, at each level, as completely as the material allows. Interpretation asks what those measurements mean together, which of them move under treatment, and whether any of it predicts what happens to the patient.

Characterisation

  1. Cancer genomics

    Mutation, copy number and mutational signatures across a cohort, and how they differ between patient populations.

  2. Single-cell biology

    Tumour, immune and stromal compartments separated out of what bulk tissue averages away.

  3. Spatial biology

    Where a cell sat in the tissue, and which neighbours it was close enough to signal to.

  4. Proteogenomics

    Protein and phosphoprotein evidence tied back to the genome and transcriptome that produced it.

Interpretation

  1. Multi-omics integration

    Models that hold several assays in one space, so a genomic change can be followed into protein.

  2. Tumour evolution

    Primary and recurrent tissue from one patient, read as a sequence rather than two snapshots.

  3. Precision oncology

    Stratification that has to reappear in a cohort we did not train on before we believe it.

  4. Translational work

    Studies designed with clinicians, released afterwards as software other groups can run.

Cancer Cell, 2024 · Kim, Migliozzi, Koo H et al.

Integrated proteogenomic characterization of glioblastoma evolution

An integrative proteogenomic analysis of longitudinal glioblastoma pairs. A highly proliferative state at diagnosis gives way, at recurrence, to neuronal transition and synaptogenic pathways, marked by post-translational activation of WNT/PCP signalling and BRAF kinase.

Single-cell analysis reveals a longitudinal trajectory of meningioma evolution and heterogeneity

Single-nuclei sequencing of matched primary and recurrent meningiomas, following how tumour cells change between operations and how they interact with the immune cells around them.

10.1038/s41467-025-60653-0

IGFBP5 is an ROR1 ligand promoting glioblastoma invasion via ROR1/HER2-CREB signaling axis

IGFBP5 identified as a ligand for ROR1 that drives invasion of glioblastoma stem-like cells. The screen that found it intersects genes up in invasive stem-like cells with genes prognostic in TCGA lower-grade glioma.

10.1038/s41467-023-37306-1

Mutation, copy number, RNA, protein and phosphoprotein data from the same tumours, organised into seven molecular subtypes of medulloblastoma.

Projects

Work in progress

Three questions the group is pursuing, each growing out of published work. The drawings are schematics of the question, not results.

Project 01 · Tumour evolution

What changes between the first operation and the one after it

A recurrent tumour is not a new tumour. Which of its features were selected by the treatment, and which were already there at diagnosis, below what the first assay could see?

Disease
Glioblastoma, meningioma
Layers
WGS / WES · RNA-seq · proteome · phosphoproteome, on matched primary and recurrent tissue
Approach
Paired variant calling, clonal deconvolution, then the same differential test applied layer by layer
Builds on
Cancer Cell 2024; Nature Communications 2025

Project 02 · Single-cell and spatial

Which cells sit next to which, and whether the arrangement matters

Counting cell types tells you what is present. It does not tell you that the immune cells never got past the rim — for that, the coordinates have to be kept.

Disease
Meningioma, glioblastoma
Layers
scRNA-seq · snRNA-seq · spatial transcriptomics
Approach
Reference-based annotation, neighbourhood graphs, recurrent-niche detection
Builds on
Nature Communications 2025

Project 03 · Precision oncology

A signature that has to survive a cohort it has never seen

Almost any integrated model separates the patients it was fitted on. The part worth publishing is the run against held-out data, and that run happens once.

Disease
Glioblastoma, medulloblastoma, gastric cancer
Layers
Genome · transcriptome · proteome · clinical outcome
Approach
Regularised models, nested cross-validation, frozen before the validation cohort is opened
Builds on
Genome Medicine 2023; Exp. & Mol. Medicine 2026

Joining

A note for people thinking of joining

If the work above is the kind of thing you want to spend a few years on, please write.

The group takes students from biology, from statistics and from computer science, and it does not much matter which door you come through; whichever half you are missing gets learned in the first year, with help. What is hard to teach is the reflex to stop when a number looks too good and go back to find out why, so that is mostly what I look for.

In practice you would spend your first months on one dataset, learning the tools that dataset needs, and by the end of the year you would own a question that is yours rather than mine.

There is no deadline and no form. Send an email with three things: the part of the research that interests you and why, one analysis you did yourself including what went wrong with it, and a CV or transcript. Every message gets an answer.

— Harim Koo
